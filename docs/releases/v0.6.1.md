Atlantis Native 0.6.1: Atlantis III fixes, first of all the Hoggar's
remote control, GOG.com's English, German and Spanish editions of
Atlantis III, and finer exactness for the first two games and the web
version.

**In 0.6.0 the Hoggar's remote control was blank and its buttons did not
respond, so you could not travel to Bagdad or Paleo. 0.6.1 fixes this.
Your saves still work: load, take the remote from your bag, click Bagdad
or Paleo, then go on.**

You need your own copy of the games: Atlantis Native contains no game
data. Play the three games in your browser at
https://ripleyxlr8.github.io/atlantis-native/ or
https://ripleyxlr8.itch.io/atlantis-native.

The fixes below come from automated audits of the port against the
original programs: each difference found was checked against the
original's code and its run under emulation, and the port now does what
the original does, with a test that replays it.

## New in 0.6.1

### Atlantis III: The New World

- **The remote control works.** In chapter 5 the Hoggar's remote was
  not drawn and no click reached its buttons, so the game could not go
  on to Bagdad or Paleo. It is now drawn and clicked as in the original,
  with its buttons' animation. The skull screen also draws the skull now.
- **No more soft-lock after Escape in a puzzle.** The original ignores
  every key while a puzzle's animation holds the input (the spinning top,
  a corridor door, the merchant, the tiger's stone, the fisherman's scale,
  the tower's demon). The port opened the menu there, and a load or a new
  game from it left every click ignored. Escape and Alt+Enter now do
  nothing at those moments, as in the original.
- **The spear stays in the bag at Paleo's hunt.** Closing the bag or the
  subject menu with the right button left the button set, so the next
  frame of the hunt ended it and took the spear away. The right button
  now also opens the bag while the view turns, as in the original.
- **GOG.com's English, German and Spanish editions** play, besides the
  French one: GOG.com ships the same program in the four languages, and
  the language is read from the installation. The game library names it
  ("Atlantis III, GOG.com, German"). German texts use the original's
  Central European character set.
- **The click on the in-game menu's Back button** no longer acts in the
  view behind it: 0.6.0 listed it as a known difference not yet checked;
  the original never does it, and the port now matches it.
- Smaller differences fixed:
  - the music stops under the cinematics, and comes back after them;
  - the camera of one of Rhea's dialogs;
  - saves are stamped with the local date and time, quick saves included,
    and print a value of -0.0 as the original does;
  - the play time counters of `Atlantis3.cfg` grow with each player's
    time;
  - with `WarpRes` 1024, the game looks for the original's 1024 / 800
    folders, which GOG.com does not ship: keep 512, as the
    [player's guide](https://github.com/ripleyXLR8/atlantis-native/blob/main/docs/GUIDE.md#atlantis-iii)
    says.

### Atlantis: The Lost Tales

A finer pass on how the original plays, from an automated audit of the
first game:

- **Two new bug fixes, on by default** (off with `--original`, and each
  can be turned off alone in the options):
  - `fix_puzzle_escape`: Escape in a puzzle does nothing at all. The
    original remembers it until the puzzle is left, so the hexagon puzzle
    then refuses its right clicks and the first line after the puzzle
    stops as soon as it starts.
  - `fix_gameover_puzzle`: after a game over on a timer, or the in-game
    menu's LOAD or QUIT, the game goes straight on. The original still
    acts on what is under the pointer: a puzzle there may open after the
    game-over picture, and the picture shows a second time.
- **A third one, `fix_cinema_sound`**, on by default and off with
  `--original`: in the original a cinematic that follows another one
  whose sound still plays is silent; the fix keeps its sound.
- **The sounds**: a fresh start has the original's volumes; the effects,
  voices and music start and fade at the original's levels; voice lines
  play at the original's rate; a cinematic's sound follows the original's
  rules.
- **The input**: a click is handled at the moment the original handles
  it; Escape during a turn or in the subject menu has the original's
  effects on the next line or video; a click, right click or Escape made
  at the very end of a move no longer acts in the new place.
- **The scripts and moves**: the music, chapter changes and triggers of
  the section walk; the panorama a move loads; the checkpoints' saved
  path; the clocks after a load from the menu.
- **Subtitles** are off at first, as at every start of the original;
  with `--original` they start off whatever `settings.ini` keeps.
- The arrow keys that turn the view, and the Enter / Up / Down keys of
  the load list, are the port's additions: they now have their own
  switch, `arrow_keys`, on by default and off with `--original`.
- `fix_head_first_frame` is removed: the original never shows the frame
  it fixed. A `settings.ini` line for it is ignored.
- The options' "Choose the bug fixes..." box takes 24, 20 or 18-pixel
  rows to fit its longer list, so it looks slightly tighter in some
  editions.

### Atlantis II

Three input details found by the audit of the second game:

- in the spider web puzzle, the cursor over a spider is the character
  cursor, as in the original;
- a click made while the in-game menu slides is kept and acts when the
  slide ends (a double click on the disc opens the load page), as in the
  original;
- a click made just before Escape ends a dialog acts in the world, as in
  the original.

### Web

- The first game runs at the right speed on high-refresh screens.
- The first game pauses while its tab is hidden, as the other two do.
- The first game's sound starts on a key press as well as on a click.
- With the pointer locked, the mouse moves as it should, with no extra
  acceleration.
- Atlantis II takes the pointer back after its menus.
- In full screen in Chrome and Edge, Escape reaches the game.
